# The Quiet Move

**Audience:** everybody already ordering — by comment on the weekly post, or by
phone.
**Objective:** five real orders through the app by the end of week 2, without
anyone being asked twice.
**Volume:** three posts in six weeks, plus two lines added to the weekly menu
post.
**Runs:** from week 1, continuously. It does not stop when the acquisition
campaign starts.

---

## The rule this campaign is built on

> **The comments never close.**

Not during the campaign, not after it, not once half the orders are coming
through the app. If someone comments "2 schnitzel for Wednesday please", that is
an order, and it is taken the way it has always been taken.

This is not a concession, it is the strategy. The moment ordering by comment
becomes second-class, the app stops being a convenience offered and starts being
a hoop, and a three-year relationship is worth more than a tidy orders table. If
the app is better, it will win on its own — and if it does not win, that is
information about the app, not about the customers.

So: no deadline for switching. No "last week for comment orders". No reply that
says *you can do that on the website now.*

## Why anyone would move

Honest list. Everything here is true today and checkable by the person reading
it.

| What they get | Why it is actually worth something |
|---|---|
| **A confirmation email** | What they ordered, what it costs, when to collect, and the reference to put in the e-transfer. A comment gives them none of that, and they currently reconstruct it by scrolling |
| **They stop waiting for a reply** | A comment is a request until Derek gets to it. An order on the app is an order the moment it is placed, and it holds stock |
| **The whole week at once** | Every day, every dish, prices, what is sold out. The post shows the week too, but the app shows what is *left* |
| **It works at eleven at night** | Which is when people actually remember about supper. Up to 22:00 it is an order; after that it is a request, and the app says which |
| **Delivery is worked out for them** | Type the postal code, get a yes or a no and a fee. No asking, no waiting, no "do you come out to Bridgeport?" |

**Not on the list, deliberately:** that it is easier for the kitchen. It is, and
that is not the customer's problem, and saying so out loud invites the very fair
reply that they were not the ones with the problem.

## The channel that does the work

Not a post. The **two lines at the bottom of the weekly menu post**, which goes
out anyway, to exactly the right people, at the moment they are deciding what to
order.

```
Order in the comments as always, or here if it's easier: dinnerbyderek.ca
Either one reaches me.
```

Add it to every menu post from week 1 and never change it. It is offered every
single week without anyone being asked once, which is the whole trick — the
frequency comes from the post that already exists, so the campaign itself stays
nearly silent.

"Either one reaches me" is the load-bearing line. It is the sentence that stops
this reading as a migration.

## The three posts

Spread across six weeks. Each one says a single thing and then stops. Full text
in [COPY-BANK.md → The Quiet Move](COPY-BANK.md#the-quiet-move).

| | Week | Post | The one thing it says |
|---|---|---|---|
| **R1** | 1 | *There's a website now* | It exists, it does what the comments do, the comments still work |
| **R2** | 3 | *You get an email now* | The single benefit that is genuinely theirs |
| **R3** | 5 | *Put it on your home screen* | For the ones who have used it twice and would use it weekly |

Three posts in six weeks is roughly one mention per fortnight against a Page
that posts several times a week. That ratio is the definition of not pushy, and
it is deliberate rather than lazy.

**If a post underperforms, it is not repeated.** There is no follow-up, no
"in case you missed it". The weekly two lines carry the campaign; the three
posts are there to explain it once each.

## The bag

Every order handed over gets a business card in it. The card already exists,
it is already printed, and its QR already resolves to the live site.

This is the highest-conversion surface in either campaign, and it costs nothing:
the person holding it has just been handed supper they are pleased about, by a
person they like, and they are not scrolling past it in a feed. See
[PRINT.md → The card in the bag](PRINT.md#the-card-in-the-bag).

Say one sentence when you hand it over, or say nothing. Both are fine. What is
not fine is a pitch at the pickup window — that is a captive audience and this
campaign does not use one.

## What to do when someone orders on the app for the first time

This is the moment the campaign either compounds or doesn't.

1. **Notice.** The dashboard shows it, and since 31 August the email does too.
2. **Say something, personally, once.** Not a marketing reply — a normal one.
   "Got it, thanks — that came through nicely. Wednesday at half five then."
   Full wording in [COPY-BANK.md → Replies](COPY-BANK.md#replies-and-small-print).
3. **Ask what was annoying.** Once, to the first five people only. "Anything on
   that form that was a nuisance?" They will tell you, and they are the only
   people who will tell you kindly. This is the actual purpose of the two-week
   gate in [STRATEGY.md → Sequencing](STRATEGY.md#sequencing).
4. **Fix what they say**, before the acquisition campaign starts. Notes go in
   [MEASUREMENT.md → What the first five said](MEASUREMENT.md#what-the-first-five-said).

Do **not** announce the first order publicly. "Our first online order!" tells
three hundred regulars that nobody else has done it yet, which is an argument
against doing it.

## What would make this campaign wrong

Written down now, so it can be spotted rather than rationalised.

- **Nobody moves at all.** Six weeks, two lines a week, three posts, a card in
  every bag, and the orders table still reads a handful. Then the app is not
  better enough than a comment for these particular people, and the honest
  response is to leave the link in the footer, stop the posts, and let the
  acquisition campaign carry it — new customers have no comment-thread habit to
  overcome and will use whatever they are given.
- **They move and then come back.** Someone orders on the app once and returns
  to commenting. That is the most useful signal available, and it means
  something specific went wrong on the form. Ask them. One question.
- **Somebody feels chased.** A single "yes I saw it, I'll use it when I'm ready"
  means the volume is already too high. Drop the remaining posts and keep only
  the two lines.
