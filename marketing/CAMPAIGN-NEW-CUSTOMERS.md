# Room at the Table

**Audience:** people in the 15 Kitchener–Waterloo postal codes who have never
heard of Dinner By Derek.
**Objective:** 8–10 first-time customers by the end of week 6, from at least two
postal codes that have never ordered.
**Volume:** two posts a week, mostly in groups rather than on the Page.
**Runs:** weeks 3–6 — **held until the gate in
[STRATEGY.md → Sequencing](STRATEGY.md#sequencing) is met.**

---

## The gate, restated

Do not start this campaign until **five real orders** have gone through the app.

Not because the campaign is fragile, but because a stranger's first experience
is the only one they will give you, and right now nobody has ever completed an
order on that form who was not testing it. Regulars are forgiving beta testers
who tell you what is wrong. Strangers close the tab.

If week 2 ends short, keep running The Quiet Move and start this one a week
later. Nothing here depends on a date.

## The proposition

> Dinner By Derek is a supper club in Kitchener and Waterloo. One kitchen, a
> small menu that changes every week, cooked the day you collect it. Order by
> ten the night before; pick it up between four and seven, or have it brought to
> your door if you are in the area.

What a stranger needs, in the order they need it:

1. **What is this?** Not a restaurant. Not a meal kit — it arrives cooked. Not a
   subscription — you order the weeks you want and no others. One person cooks a
   small menu a few days a week.
2. **Is it for me?** Fifteen postal codes, pickup between four and seven,
   e-transfer or cash, no account and no app store.
3. **Is it safe?** Three years of cooking, a real name, a real phone number, and
   a menu that cannot be published until every dish on it has been
   allergen-reviewed.
4. **What do I do?** Pick a day. Order by ten the night before. Collect, or have
   it delivered.

Answer them in that order. A post that opens on point 4 is talking to somebody
who has not been given a reason to care yet.

## Where it runs

**Local Facebook groups do the heavy lifting.** Buy-and-sell, neighbourhood,
parents', newcomer and food groups across KW. That is where people who have not
heard of this are, and a recommendation in a group is read as a recommendation
rather than as an advertisement.

Two things to know before posting into any of them, both of which will
otherwise cost you a ban rather than a sale:

- **Read the group's rules and honour them.** Many KW groups allow local
  business posts only on a named day, or only in a pinned thread, or not at all.
  A post removed by a moderator is worse than no post — you lose the group
  permanently and you cannot appeal it.
- **Posting to a group cannot be automated by anything.** Meta retired the
  Groups API on 22 April 2024. Every group post in this campaign is a human
  copy-and-paste, which the app supports directly: the week page has a **Copy
  post text** button and the photos download with one tap.

Keep a list of which groups you have posted to and when, so the same group does
not get the same post twice in a month. A table to fill in is at the bottom of
[MEASUREMENT.md](MEASUREMENT.md#the-group-log).

**The Page carries two of these posts**, in weeks 3 and 5 — the "what is this"
explainer and the allergen post. Both are written to be interesting to somebody
who already orders, not just to a stranger, because the regulars are reading
them too. See [STRATEGY.md → The collision problem](STRATEGY.md#the-collision-problem).

**Print goes where the feed does not reach.** Flyers on community boards,
stickers on containers, cards handed to people who ask. [PRINT.md](PRINT.md).

## The six posts

One idea each, in the order a stranger needs them. Full text in
[COPY-BANK.md → Room at the Table](COPY-BANK.md#room-at-the-table).

| | Week | Post | Where | The one thing it says |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **A1** | 3 | *What this is* | Page + groups | A supper club in KW. Not a restaurant, not a meal kit |
| **A2** | 3 | *How it works* | Groups | Pick a day, order by ten the night before, collect or delivered |
| **A3** | 4 | *Where we deliver* | Groups | The fifteen postal codes, named. The most shareable post in the set |
| **A4** | 4 | *The allergen review* | Page + groups | The menu cannot go up until every dish has been read |
| **A5** | 5 | *The week's menu, to a room that has never seen one* | Groups | Look at the actual food |
| **A6** | 6 | *Meatless Monday and the reheat bags* | Groups | It is not one dish a night — there is range, and there is food for the freezer |

**A3 is the one to get right.** "Do you deliver to N2K?" is the question this
market asks, and a post that answers it by listing every code is the post that
gets screenshotted into another group by somebody who is not you. Fifteen codes
is a short list; print all fifteen.

## Group-post etiquette, in five lines

1. One post per group per month. Never the same post twice.
2. Answer every comment, including the ones that are only "following".
3. Never post the same text into two groups on the same day — people are in both,
   and identical text an hour apart reads as spam even when it isn't.
4. If a moderator asks you to move or remove it, do it immediately and without
   argument, and say thank you.
5. Do not comment your business under somebody else's "where can I get dinner?"
   post unless the group explicitly allows it. Where it does, answer their
   question first and mention the business second.

## What a new customer's first order must not hit

Three conditions that turn a working campaign into a bad review. Check them the
morning of every acquisition post.

| Condition | Where | What goes wrong |
|---|---|---|
| **No published week** | Dashboard → This Week | They land on "the menu for these dates isn't up yet" and never come back. Publish before posting, not after |
| **No daily cap** | Dashboard → This Week, per day | The post works, forty people order, one person cooks. See [STRATEGY.md → Capacity](STRATEGY.md#capacity-is-the-real-risk) |
| **Posting after 22:00** | The clock | The form is gone by then — it takes requests, not orders. Post in the morning, for a day that is still two days out |

That last one is worth stating positively: **the best time to post is a morning,
about a dish two or three days away.** It gives the reader a decision they can
make now and act on later, which is exactly what the cutoff is designed for.

## Handling the first stranger

A new customer's order is more fragile than a regular's. They have no
relationship to fall back on and no idea what normal looks like.

- **Confirm it like a person, not a system.** The app already sent them an
  email; what you add is that a human saw it.
- **Say what happens next, in full.** Where to come, what the building looks
  like, what to bring, what to do about payment. Things that are obvious after
  three years are not obvious on a first order.
- **If they are picking up, tell them where to park.** More first-time customers
  are lost to this than to anything on the menu.
- **Ask, once, how they found you.** Not on the form — in the confirmation
  message. It is the only source-attribution this campaign has, and it costs one
  sentence. [MEASUREMENT.md → Where did they come from](MEASUREMENT.md#where-did-they-come-from).

## What would make this campaign wrong

- **Groups get no traction and print does.** Entirely possible in a market this
  local, and it means the budget of effort moves to flyers, markets and the
  card. Watch the postal-code counts on Locations & Delivery — print and groups
  produce different geographic patterns.
- **It works too well in one week.** The cap holds the night, but the recovery
  is to *slow the posting*, not to raise the cap. A sold-out Wednesday is a good
  advertisement; a bad Wednesday is not.
- **New customers order once and never again.** That is not a marketing problem
  and no amount of posting fixes it. Stop the campaign, and find out what the
  second order was missing.
